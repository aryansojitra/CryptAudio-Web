Steganographic Workflow Architecture

End-to-end cryptographic and signal processing sequence

1

Carrier Audio Selection

Select an uncompressed 8, 16, 24, or 32-bit PCM WAV audio file to serve as the innocent carrier.

2

Key Derivation & AES-GCM

PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 derives a 256-bit key using a fresh 16-byte salt and 600,000 iterations to encrypt with AES-GCM.

3

Sample-Level LSB Framing

A 13-byte header with magic marker, CRC32, and payload size is prefixed and embedded bit-by-bit into sample LSBs.

4

Extraction & Verification

The receiver extracts LSB bits, validates the CRC32 checksum, and verifies the 16-byte GCM tag to ensure no tampering.

About CryptAudio Architecture

Academic foundations, cryptographic threat modeling, and steganographic limitations.

1. What is Audio Steganography?

Steganography is the science of concealing the existence of communication. Unlike traditional cryptography which protects message secrecy but leaves ciphertext openly visible, steganography embeds confidential information into an innocent cover media (such as audio, image, or video) so that casual observers cannot detect that secret communication is occurring.

CryptAudio Web combines both paradigms: the message is first encrypted using authenticated cryptography so that even if the stego carrier is intercepted, the adversary cannot decipher the contents without the secret key.

2. Cryptographic Security Engine

CryptAudio Web uses AES-256-GCM (Galois/Counter Mode), an authenticated encryption with associated data (AEAD) cipher standard approved by NIST:

  • Key Derivation: Passwords are transformed into 256-bit AES keys via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 utilizing 600,000 iterations and a fresh 16-byte cryptographically random salt per encryption (OWASP 2023 recommendation).
  • Fresh Nonce: A 12-byte cryptographically random nonce is generated for every message, preventing replay and keystream reuse attacks.
  • Integrity Authentication: A 128-bit (16-byte) GCM authentication tag authenticates the ciphertext and header parameters, guaranteeing message tampering detection.

3. Least Significant Bit (LSB) Embedding

Digital audio represents acoustic sound pressure levels as discrete numerical samples (e.g. 16-bit integers between -32,768 and +32,767). The Least Significant Bit (LSB) is the bit with the lowest numerical weight ($2^0 = 1$).

CryptAudio substitutes the LSB of individual audio samples with consecutive bits of the encrypted payload. Modifying an individual sample's LSB alters its amplitude by at most 1 unit in a range of tens of thousands, which falls far below the human ear's perceptual threshold (Differential Limen for Intensity).

The embedded data begins with a 13-byte protocol header: [ 'C' 'S' 'L' 'B' ] [ 1 byte Version ] [ 4 bytes Payload Length ] [ 4 bytes CRC32 ].

4. Why Uncompressed PCM WAV?

CryptAudio strictly requires uncompressed Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) in RIFF WAVE (.wav) containers. Unlike lossy formats, linear PCM stores the exact digital sample amplitudes without psychoacoustic quantization or lossy transform coding.

5. Academic Limitations & Threat Model

Important Academic Notice

LSB steganography is fragile by design and is not mathematically undetectable:

  • Lossy Recompression: Converting a stego WAV to MP3, AAC, OGG, or Opus applies psychoacoustic lossy transforms that permanently erase the embedded LSB data.
  • Audio Signal Processing: Resampling, equalization, normalization, pitch-shifting, or reverb alters sample amplitudes and corrupts the hidden bits.
  • Steganalysis Susceptibility: Statistical steganalysis techniques (such as Chi-squared testing, sample pair analysis, or higher-order Markov modeling) can detect artificial alterations to the natural LSB distribution in audio carriers.
