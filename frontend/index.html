About CryptAudio Architecture

Academic foundations, cryptographic threat modeling, and steganographic limitations.

1. What is Audio Steganography?

Steganography aims to conceal the existence of communication by embedding information inside an innocent-looking carrier. Unlike traditional cryptography which protects message secrecy but leaves ciphertext openly visible, steganography conceals the transmission itself within carrier media (such as audio waveforms). Detection remains possible through statistical analysis and steganalysis.

CryptAudio Web combines both paradigms: the confidential message is first encrypted using authenticated symmetric cryptography (AES-256-GCM) so that even if the hidden payload is discovered or extracted, an adversary cannot decipher the contents without the authorized passphrase.

A cryptographically random 12-byte nonce is generated for every encryption operation. Nonce uniqueness is essential for AES-GCM security and prevents dangerous nonce reuse. CryptAudio does not currently implement a separate message freshness or replay-detection mechanism.

4. Least Significant Bit (LSB) Embedding & Audio Format

Digital audio represents acoustic sound pressure levels as discrete numerical samples (e.g. 16-bit signed integers between −32,768 and +32,767). The Least Significant Bit (LSB) is the bit with the lowest numerical weight ($2^0 = 1$).

Changing the least significant bit generally produces a small numerical change in a PCM sample. The perceptual effect depends on the audio content and listening conditions.

CryptAudio strictly requires uncompressed Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) in RIFF WAVE (.wav) containers. Unlike lossy formats, linear PCM stores the exact digital sample amplitudes without psychoacoustic quantization or lossy transform coding.

5. Academic Limitations & Threat Model

Important Academic Notice & Threat Boundaries

LSB steganography is fragile by design and is not mathematically undetectable:

  • Lossy Recompression: Converting a stego WAV to MP3, AAC, OGG, or Opus applies psychoacoustic lossy transforms that permanently erase the embedded LSB data.
  • Audio Signal Processing: Resampling, equalization, normalization, pitch-shifting, or reverb alters sample amplitudes and corrupts the hidden bits.
  • Steganalysis Susceptibility: Statistical steganalysis techniques (such as Chi-squared testing, sample pair analysis, or higher-order Markov modeling) can detect artificial alterations to the natural LSB distribution in audio carriers.
  • Numerical vs Perceptual Metrics: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) are numerical signal-quality metrics. High numerical fidelity indicates small mathematical differences, but does not prove subjective perceptual inaudibility.
